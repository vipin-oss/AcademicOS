# AcademicOS — Persona Journeys & the Brain Home Screen

> **Author stance:** Human Experience Designer. **No architecture.** Only real university life, human workflows, and how the product feels to a person at 9 a.m. with coffee in hand.
> **Companion to:** the Product Blueprint, Object-Centric Blueprint, and Four Pillars Extension — but this document never opens the hood. It describes *life*, not *machinery*.

The university is a living organism of people juggling teaching, research, students, committees, money, and deadlines — mostly across a chaotic scatter of email, messaging apps, spreadsheets, shared drives, and paper. This document follows eight real people through their actual days, names their pain, and shows how AcademicOS (their "Brain") quietly removes it.

---

# 1. ASSISTANT PROFESSOR (AP)

*Early-career. Teaching two courses, building a research line, writing first grants, on two committees, supervising two master's students. Constantly aware that "publish or perish" starts now.*

## A Day
**Morning.** Checks phone in bed — three messaging-group pings about a class timing change. Opens laptop, digs through email for the syllabus draft a colleague promised. Spends 20 minutes finding last year's version. Skims a student's draft, makes notes in a separate doc she'll probably lose. Realises a grant pre-proposal is due Friday and panics quietly.
**Afternoon.** Teaches two back-to-back lectures. A student stays behind with a confused question about a prerequisite he never took — she promises to "send something." Office hour: five students, three unrelated requests. Tries to write the grant but is pulled into a committee meeting that runs long; action items are noted hastily on paper.
**Evening.** Exhausted. Wants to read two new papers but can't find where she saved them. Answers a few student emails. The grant pre-proposal is still a blank page. Sleeps uneasily.

## Typical Week
Lectures 6–8 hrs, 2–3 committee meetings, 4–5 office hours, grant writing in fragments, manuscript reviews for others, student supervision snatched between tasks. Weekends meant for research quietly absorbed by admin.

## Typical Semester
Weeks 1–2: syllabus finalisation, class setup. Weeks 3–10: teaching grind + mid-sem exam papers. Week 11–12: grading avalanche. Weeks 13–15: project evaluations, course reports, and a desperate push to submit a paper before the conference deadline. End-of-semester: accreditation evidence scramble.

## Typical Academic Year
Monsoon semester (teach + grant), winter break (hopefully a paper), spring semester (teach + conference travel), summer (research sprint + new grant). Annual review looms every year with a self-appraisal nobody enjoys assembling.

## Pain Points
1. Fragmented tools — class info in messaging, drafts in drive, notes on paper, deadlines in head.
2. Lost versions of syllabi, grants, manuscripts.
3. Grant deadlines sneak up; no single view of "what's due."
4. Committee action items evaporate after meetings.
5. Can't find past papers / past proposals when needed.
6. Student follow-ups slip through.
7. No memory of "how we ran this last time."

## How AcademicOS solves it (experience)
- **One Brain, one morning Briefing** tells her: grant pre-proposal due Friday, two student messages need replies, committee action assigned to her is open.
- **Her Teaching Workspace** holds every syllabus, lecture, and student in one place — last year's version is one click away, and the current one is always the current one.
- **The Grant Workspace** auto-builds a timeline the moment she starts a grant: proposal doc, budget, report dates, reminders — she never reconstructs it.
- **Meeting outcomes** become trackable items she can see and close, not scribbles.
- **Memory** recalls the last grant she wrote and pre-fills the boilerplate; recalls the papers she saved and resurfaces them when relevant.
- **Her Inbox** gathers every ask directed at her — student, committee, deadline — so nothing hides in a group chat.
- **Proactive nudges**: "Two papers on your method published this month," "Confererence X deadline in 20 days."

## Brain Home Screen
- *Brain Briefing* (today in 4 lines)
- *My Day* strip: lectures, office hour, meeting, grant reminder
- *Open Items* (from Inbox): approvals, replies, actions
- *Teaching* card: "Lecture 3 tomorrow — 2 students flagged at-risk"
- *Research* card: "Grant pre-proposal due Fri · 2 new relevant papers"
- *Timeline* peek: this week at a glance

## First 30 seconds after login
"Good morning. You teach at 10 and 2; office hour at 3. Your grant pre-proposal is due Friday — last year's draft is attached. Two students messaged you; one needs the prerequisite material you promised. Committee action 'send venue quote' is still open. Two new papers match your method."

# 2. ASSOCIATE PROFESSOR

*Mid-career. Leads a small lab, supervises 4–5 PhDs, teaches less, writes bigger grants, sits on more committees, reviews constantly.*

## A Day
**Morning.** Briefing: a PhD student's experiment is stuck; a co-author sent a manuscript for comments; a grant review is due. Clears the manuscript review first (he's reliable). Reads the student's panic email, replies with a concrete fix.
**Afternoon.** Lab meeting — 6 students present. He tracks who's on track, who's drifting. A funding call dropped yesterday; he senses a fit with a student's project and makes a note to discuss. Two manuscript reviews and a departmental form eat the rest.
**Evening.** Writes with a postdoc on a joint paper. Realises a collaboration from two years ago has unpublished data that fits — wishes he could recall exactly what they measured.

## Typical Week
Lab meeting, 3–4 PhD syncs, manuscript reviews (he's in demand), grant writing, 1–2 courses, committee work, networking emails.

## Typical Semester
Steady research supervision; mid-sem and end-sem grading; a conference or two; grant submissions clustered before calls close; PhD progress reviews.

## Typical Academic Year
Two teaching semesters + heavy summer research. Annual: promotions committee, lab growth, big-grant cycles, PhD graduations.

## Pain Points
1. Supervising many students without losing anyone.
2. Forgetting prior collaborations' details ("what data did we collect?").
3. Review load competes with his own writing.
4. Scattered grant calls; misses fits.
5. Committee forms and follow-ups.

## How AcademicOS solves it
- **Research Workspace** shows every PhD, their Tasks, and drift signals — he sees who's stuck before the panic email.
- **Memory** recalls the old collaboration's dataset and resurfaces it exactly when relevant.
- **Inbox** separates "reviews due" from "student needs you" so he protects writing time.
- **Grant Workspace** watches funding calls and flags matches to his lab's areas — opportunities find him.
- **Meeting outcomes** and committee forms become trackable, not lost.

## Brain Home Screen
- *Brain Briefing*
- *Lab* card: "3 on track · 1 stuck (experiment) · 1 milestone due"
- *Reviews due* chip
- *Grants* card: "1 call matches your area — closes in 18 days"
- *Writing* card: "Joint paper with postdoc — 2 sections open"
- *Timeline*

## First 30 seconds
"Morning. Lab meeting at 11 — one student is stuck on the experiment; talking points ready. Manuscript from Dr. Rao needs your comments by Thursday. A SERB call matches your lab's method (closes 18 days). The 2022 collaboration dataset you mentioned is here."

# 3. PROFESSOR

*Senior. Chairs a major grant, heads a large lab, on the senate and two national committees, teaches little, advises broadly, represents the institution publicly.*

## A Day
**Morning.** Briefing: a journalist asked for comment; a senate agenda dropped; a PhD scholar defends next week; a conflict between two junior faculty needs a quiet word. Dictates a few replies, delegates the journalist query to an office assistant via a clear task.
**Afternoon.** Strategy meeting for the centre he directs. Reviews a flagship manuscript. A junior colleague asks for a letter of support — he approves with context. Senate prep: skims past resolutions to stay consistent.
**Evening.** Reads for pleasure-adjacent scholarship; mentors a former student by message; plans next quarter's big push.

## Typical Week
High-level meetings, grant oversight, mentoring, manuscript/letter approvals, senate/committee, selective teaching, public roles.

## Typical Semester
Oversees multiple grants and labs; mid/end-sem light teaching; defences; strategic hiring; accreditation leadership.

## Typical Academic Year
Big-grant cycles, centre strategy, PhD graduations, national committee travel, institutional planning, annual reviews of many people.

## Pain Points
1. Too many approvals/requests; context-switching cost.
2. Forgetting past institutional decisions ("did senate already decide this?").
3. Conflicts and people issues needing memory of history.
4. Junior faculty waiting on him.
5. Public/institutional memory he's expected to hold.

## How AcademicOS solves it
- **His Inbox** is triaged: approvals, delegations, and "needs your judgement" rise to top; routine drops down.
- **Memory** recalls past senate resolutions and decisions so he stays consistent without reconstructing.
- **Committee Workspaces** keep agendas, minutes, and action items; he opens a meeting and sees exactly what was decided and what's open.
- **Delegation** is one tap — a request becomes a tracked task for the right person, with context, not a vague message.
- **Proactive insights** flag conflicts or at-risk situations early, giving him time to act humanely.

## Brain Home Screen
- *Brain Briefing* (strategic)
- *Approvals* card: "3 need you — 1 letter of support, 1 budget, 1 hire"
- *Centre/Grants* card: "Flagship grant on track · 1 milestone next week"
- *People* card: "2 junior faculty awaiting your word · 1 PhD defence scheduled"
- *Committees* card: "Senate agenda ready · 1 open decision from March to revisit"
- *Timeline* (quarter view)

## First 30 seconds
"Good morning. Three approvals await: a letter of support, a budget transfer, a hire. Senate agenda is ready — one open decision from March resurfaces for consistency. A PhD defends next week. Two junior colleagues are waiting on you; one needs a quiet conversation."

# 4. RESEARCH SCHOLAR (Postdoc / Senior Researcher)

*Deep in research. Publishes, experiments, collaborates, reviews. Little teaching. Career-building.*

## A Day
**Morning.** Briefing: a paper she co-authored got cited; a reviewer comment arrived on her submission; a dataset from a collaborator is ready. Reads the comment — it's fair; she starts a revision.
**Afternoon.** Runs analysis; hits a methods snag; searches her saved readings for a technique she half-remembers. Writes the revision. A collaborator messages about a joint submission.
**Evening.** Reads new literature the system flagged as relevant; drafts a new idea note; plans the week's experiments.

## Typical Week
Analysis, writing, reading, collaboration calls, manuscript submissions, reviews, lab time.

## Typical Semester
Submission cycles, revision rounds, a conference, progress with the PI, maybe a course assist.

## Typical Academic Year
Multiple submissions, a big conference, grant applications (fellowships), annual progress review, job-market prep late in the cycle.

## Pain Points
1. Losing track of which manuscript version is current.
2. Forgetting saved readings / techniques.
3. Reviewer comments scattered across email.
4. Missing relevant new papers.
5. Collaboration handoffs ("did they send the data?").

## How AcademicOS solves it
- **Her Research Workspace** keeps every manuscript as one living thing with a clear current version and full history.
- **Memory** recalls the technique she half-remembered and the readings she saved — surfaced exactly when stuck.
- **Reviewer comments** live on the paper, threaded, with status — never lost in email.
- **The system watches new literature** and brings relevant papers to her, not the other way around.
- **Collaboration handoffs** are visible: the dataset from her collaborator is right there, linked.

## Brain Home Screen
- *Brain Briefing*
- *Manuscripts* card: "1 revision open (reviewer comment fair) · 1 under review"
- *Reading* card: "3 new papers match your method"
- *Collaboration* card: "Dataset from Dr. Rao ready"
- *Writing* strip: sections in progress
- *Timeline*

## First 30 seconds
"Morning. Your submission got a reviewer comment — it's fair; revision ready to start. A collaborator's dataset is available. Three new papers match your method. One manuscript is under review; no action needed yet."

# 5. PhD STUDENT

*Years of coursework → research → qualifying exam → publications → thesis → job market. Lives in uncertainty and deadlines.*

## A Day
**Morning.** Briefing: qualifying exam proposal draft due; advisor commented on his chapter; a seminar today. Reads the advisor's comment, makes the edit.
**Afternoon.** Class, then lab. Experiment partially works; he logs it. Attends seminar, takes notes. A senior student shares a useful paper.
**Evening.** Writes the proposal section; fights distraction; sets a reminder. Worries quietly about timeline.

## Typical Week
Classes (early), research, advisor meetings, seminars, TA duties, writing, exam prep.

## Typical Semester
Coursework (yr1) → research ramp → qual exam → publishing → thesis writing. Always: proposals, deadlines, TA grading.

## Typical Academic Year
Year 1 coursework; Year 2–3 research + exams + first papers; Year 4 thesis + job market; summer research sprints.

## Pain Points
1. Losing advisor feedback across email/docs.
2. Not knowing if he's on track.
3. Forgetting seminar/material insights.
4. TA deadlines competing with research.
5. Version chaos of thesis chapters.

## How AcademicOS solves it
- **His Student Workspace** holds his plan, deadlines, advisor notes, and progress in one place.
- **Advisor feedback** lives on the chapter, threaded and dated — never lost.
- **The system shows his trajectory** ("qual exam in 6 weeks, proposal draft due Friday") so he's never surprised.
- **Seminar notes and shared papers** are captured and resurfaced when relevant to his work.
- **Thesis** is one living document with clear versions; TA tasks are separate but visible.

## Brain Home Screen
- *Brain Briefing* (reassuring, clear)
- *My Path* card: "Qual exam in 6 weeks · proposal draft due Fri"
- *Advisor* card: "1 comment on Chapter 2 — addressed?"
- *TA* card: "Grading due Mon"
- *Reading* card: "Paper shared by senior student"
- *Timeline*

## First 30 seconds
"Hi. Your qualifying-exam proposal draft is due Friday — you're on track. Your advisor commented on Chapter 2; it's a small edit. A senior student shared a paper that fits your method. TA grading is due Monday — plan around it."

# 6. DEPARTMENT OFFICE (Office Superintendent / Admin Staff)

*The engine room. Schedules, meetings, purchases, grants admin, student records, events, accreditation paperwork. Always firefighting.*

## A Day
**Morning.** Briefing: Chair wants the timetable finalised; a purchase needs the HoD's sign; NAAC evidence folder is missing two documents; a faculty member needs a letter drafted. Starts the timetable, sends the purchase for approval.
**Afternoon.** Faculty meeting — takes minutes, assigns action items. A vendor calls about the delayed equipment. A student arrives for a certificate; she hunts the record.
**Evening.** Compiles the NAAC evidence, still short two docs; emails the responsible faculty. Updates the purchase tracker.

## Typical Week
Timetables, meetings + minutes, purchases (many), grant paperwork, student records, event logistics, accreditation, constant follow-ups.

## Typical Semester
Timetable at start; exams + seating; event season; accreditation windows; student record updates.

## Typical Academic Year
Admissions, new-student onboarding, two exam cycles, annual accreditation (NAAC/NBA/SAR), budget close, event calendar.

## Pain Points
1. Chasing faculty for signatures and documents.
2. Losing which purchase/approval is pending with whom.
3. NAAC/NBA/SAR evidence scatter — last-minute scramble.
4. Meeting action items forgotten by everyone.
5. Student records hard to find fast.
6. No single view of "what's stuck."

## How AcademicOS solves it
- **The Administration Workspace** shows every purchase, approval, and document with status and owner — she sees at a glance what's stuck with whom.
- **Approval flows** route automatically to the right person and remind them; she's no longer the manual nag.
- **Accreditation Workspaces** auto-collect evidence from across the department — the system tells her exactly which two documents are missing and who should provide them.
- **Meeting minutes** become tracked action items everyone can see and close.
- **Student records** are one search away, with a clear audit of every action.
- **Her Inbox** is the single "what's waiting" surface, ranked by urgency.

## Brain Home Screen
- *Brain Briefing* (operational)
- *Pending Approvals* card: "2 with HoD · 1 with Dean"
- *NAAC* card: "Criterion 3 missing 2 documents — owners notified"
- *Meetings* card: "Faculty meeting today — 4 actions open"
- *Purchases* card: "3 in pipeline · 1 delayed (vendor)"
- *Students* card: "Certificate request pending record pull"
- *Timeline* (week)

## First 30 seconds
"Morning. Two approvals wait with the HoD, one with the Dean. NAAC Criterion 3 is missing two documents — the owners were just reminded. Faculty meeting today has 4 open actions. One equipment purchase is delayed; vendor called. A student certificate needs a record pull."

## Typical Semester
Timetable oversight, mid/end-sem coordination, hiring pushes, accreditation windows, student welfare spikes.

## Typical Academic Year
Admissions, budgeting cycles, faculty reviews, accreditation, strategic planning, graduation.

## Pain Points

2. Budget overspend discovered late.
3. Accreditation self-study assembled under time pressure.

5. Student issues surfacing too late.

- **Accreditation** evidence is continuously collected, so the self-study is mostly assembled, not scramble-built.

- **Proactive insights** flag at-risk students and brewing conflicts before they erupt.

# THE BRAIN HOME SCREEN — DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Across all eight personas, the Brain is **not a dashboard of charts**. It is a calm, spoken-to-you briefing of *your* world, composed from your Workspaces, your Inbox, your Timeline, and the institution's memory. Rules we held to:

- **One greeting, one paragraph.** The Brain talks to you like a competent chief-of-staff, not a wall of widgets.
- **Today first, context second.** What's due / who needs you / what's stuck — then the standing cards.
- **Persona-shaped, not template.** An AP sees teaching + grant; a Dean sees departments + risk; a student sees path + advisor.
- **Quiet by default.** Insights are ranked; only what matters shows. Nothing nags.
- **Everything is one tap from its source.** A mention is one click from the person, the document, the meeting.

# FIRST 30 SECONDS AFTER LOGIN — WHAT MUST APPEAR

Regardless of persona, the first half-minute always answers four human questions, in order:

1. **"What's happening today?"** — lectures/meetings/office hours/deadlines on the day.
2. **"Who needs me?"** — replies, approvals, student/faculty flags, delegations.
3. **"What's due or stuck?"** — near deadlines, open actions, blocked items, missing documents.
4. **"What should I notice?"** — one or two calm, relevant insights (a new paper, an opportunity, a risk).

Then, and only then, the standing cards for that persona appear. The greeting is always plain language, never a notification bomb. The person should close the first 30 seconds feeling *lighter* — "the Brain has the chaos; I just need to act."
